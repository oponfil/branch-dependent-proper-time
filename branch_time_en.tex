\title[Branch-Dependent Proper Time]{Branch-Dependent Proper Time}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Oleg} \sur{Ponfilenok}}\email{ponfil@gmail.com}

\affil*[1]{\orgname{Independent researcher}, \orgaddress{\country{Vietnam}}}

\abstract{We propose tying the fundamental scale of gravitationally induced space-time blurring to \emph{proper time} rather than to spatial length, and postulate that, under an ensemble reading of the quantum state in which each branch carries its own world line, proper-time fluctuations are statistically independent across branches. Interference is sensitive to the \emph{difference} of branch phase contributions; with independent clocks this difference accumulates at the frequency $\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}=E_{\mathrm{br}}/\hbar$. For a composite system we define this branch-sensitive energy operationally: the rest energy of each particle species is weighted by the trace distance between the one-particle reduced density matrices of the two branches; a fully distinguishable which-path carrier gives $E_{\mathrm{br}}=mc^2$, while strictly internal branches with a common spatial-motional state give $E_{\mathrm{br}}=\Delta E$. This yields $V/V_0=\exp(-\chi)$ with $\chi=(\omega_{\mathrm{eff}})^2(\xi\,t_P^2\,t)^{2/3}$ for a branch pair with $E_a\approx E_b$ (for internal branches with $E_a=0$ an additional factor $1/2$ appears); for a centre-of-mass interferometer with fully distinguishable packets $\chi=(mc^2/\hbar)^2(\xi\,t_P^2\,t)^{2/3}$, where $t$ is the superposition time and $\xi=\mathcal O(1)$ is a numerical coefficient. The scaling form $\chi\propto m^2 t^{2/3}$ follows from the postulates; the overall coefficient carries an $\mathcal O(1)$ uncertainty. The cubic scaling is borrowed from the K\'arolyh\'azy relation; the decoherence mechanism differs from Di\'osi--Penrose collapse. Confronted with sodium-nanoparticle interferometry of the Vienna group~\cite{arndt2025}, this gives an upper bound $\xi\lesssim1.1$. A decisive test is near: at $m\sim1$~MDa and $v\approx25$~m/s the model predicts $\chi\gg1$ and practically complete loss of interference, whereas the standard quantum model of the setup at the same parameters expects measurable fringes with ordinary contrast.}

\keywords{proper time, branch clock independence, K\'arolyh\'azy uncertainty, decoherence, matter-wave interferometry, Margolus--Levitin theorem, quantum gravity}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The status of the wave function---an ontic object or a computational bookkeeping of an ensemble of possibilities---remains unsettled. We adopt the second, ensemble reading~\cite{ballentine1970,home1992}: $\lvert\psi\rangle=\sum_i c_i\lvert i\rangle$ catalogues distinct \emph{variants} of the system, and the squared amplitudes are the relative weights of those variants. This is a deliberately minimal ontology; the substantive postulates on independent branch clocks (\S~\ref{sec:postulates}) are added separately, once gravity enters the picture.

In the relational view of time, a clock is a physical subsystem and ``time'' is a correlation within the total state~\cite{pagewootters}. If each branch is a genuine variant of the system, then it carries its own world line and hence its own proper time. Penrose reaches a structurally identical statement from the opposite direction: each component of the superposition sources its own space-time, and ``since there is no canonical way of relating the time parameters in the two space-times, a fundamental uncertainty in the time-translation operator arises''~\cite{penrose1996}. Consistently with this, proper time accumulates along a world line and is not fixed by a single common scale for distinct four-dimensional paths: in an interferometer with different trajectories one cannot, without an additional postulate, impose a single common proper-time parameter on all branches. While the system is in a superposition of paths, interference requires a coherent relative phase between them; branch-independent proper-time fluctuations (postulates~P2--P3 below) introduce a random shift of this phase and bound the visibility from above, $V/V_0\le e^{-\chi}$ (see~\eqref{eq:chi})---this is a prediction of an interference limit under path-dependent proper time, not a denial of amplitude superposition. After a which-path registration, a single scenario and a single realized world line are fixed; the $|c_i|^2$ remain the weights of the outcomes. The mechanism below is pure dephasing between branches: populations are not redistributed, and no which-path information is recorded.

\section{Postulates}\label{sec:postulates}

We introduce the key notation:
\begin{itemize}
\item $t$ --- the time the system spends in superposition (a proper-time interval along the world line);
\item $\delta t$ --- the scale of proper-time uncertainty over the interval $t$; $\delta t_a$ --- the realized shift on branch $a$ (P3), $\mathrm{Var}(\delta t_a)=\delta t^2$;
\item $t_P=\sqrt{\hbar G/c^5}$ --- the Planck time;
\item $E_{\mathrm{br}}$ --- the energy of the \emph{split} subsystem in the interfering branch pair;
\item $\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}=E_{\mathrm{br}}/\hbar$ --- the corresponding effective phase-noise frequency;
\item $m$ --- the carrier mass in the which-path case (then $E_{\mathrm{br}}=mc^2$, $\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}=\omega_C=mc^2/\hbar$);
\item $\xi=\mathcal O(1)$ --- a dimensionless numerical coefficient.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{(P1) Branch structure.}
A branch is a state component differing in a \emph{physical degree of freedom} that participates in interference: a spatial-motional state (which-path or current), or an internal eigenstate of $H_{\mathrm{int}}$ (spin, a hyperfine or optical level, a qubit). One connected and indistinguishable wave packet carries one clock; branching occurs when evolution splits the state into distinguishable components, and ceases upon their recombination or which-path registration.

To prevent the choice of the ``split subsystem'' from depending on an arbitrary grouping of a composite system, we fix an operational rule. For particles of species $s$, let $\gamma^{(1)}_{a,s}$ and $\gamma^{(1)}_{b,s}$ be the one-particle reduced density matrices of the branches, normalized by $\operatorname{Tr}\gamma^{(1)}_{a,s}=\operatorname{Tr}\gamma^{(1)}_{b,s}=N_s$. The number of one-particle states distinguishable between the branches and the corresponding branch-sensitive energy are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_s(a,b)=\frac12\left\|\gamma^{(1)}_{a,s}-\gamma^{(1)}_{b,s}\right\|_1,
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{br}}^{\mathrm{mot}}=c^2\sum_s m_s\,\mathcal D_s(a,b).
\label{eq:ebr_dist}
\end{equation}
Here $0\le\mathcal D_s\le N_s$; the definition is basis-invariant and applies to identical particles~\cite{korsbakken2007}. For fully distinguishable spatial packets of every constituent, $\mathcal D_s=N_s$, and hence $E_{\mathrm{br}}^{\mathrm{mot}}=Mc^2$. States common to both branches contribute zero. If only an internal level differs while the spatial-motional carrier $|C\rangle$ is common, we use the transition energy $E_{\mathrm{br}}^{\mathrm{int}}=|\Delta E|$, not the rest energy of the common carrier. If both types of difference are present, their branch-sensitive contributions are added.

\subsection{(P2) Magnitude of the proper-time fluctuation.}
The accumulated uncertainty of the proper-time interval $t$ measured along a world line is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\delta t^3=\xi\,t_P^2\,t,}
\label{eq:dt}
\end{equation}
with $\xi=\mathcal O(1)$. Equation~(\ref{eq:dt}) is the temporal form of the K\'arolyh\'azy uncertainty relation~\cite{karolyhazy1966}; the same cubic scaling is consistent with the clock arguments of Salecker--Wigner~\cite{saleckerwigner}, Ng--van Dam~\cite{ngvandam1995,ngvandam1994}, and the holographic bound on space-time uncertainty~\cite{ng2005foam}. We do not fix the numerical coefficient $\xi$ in the postulate: in these works it is likewise never pinned down more precisely than to $\mathcal O(1)$.

We stress that gravity is essential: without the ``not a black hole'' constraint the effect vanishes ($\delta t\to0$ as $m\to\infty$). The rest mass is equally essential: the uncertainty $\delta t$ is defined along a world line with nonzero proper time; without rest mass there is no proper time. For a photon $m=0$, $\mathrm{d}\tau=0$, and the entire mechanism does not apply to a light-like carrier. The exponent $1/3$ and the Planck scale $t_P^2$ are gravitational in origin; the postulate is formulated along a world line, without explicitly invoking spatial length.

\subsection{(P3) Branch clock independence.}
For two branches $a,b$ (in the sense of~P1) over the superposition interval $t$ (the same as in~\eqref{eq:dt}), the realized shifts $\delta t_a,\delta t_b$ are independent, centered Gaussian random variables of accumulated proper time with $\mathrm{Var}(\delta t_a)=\mathrm{Var}(\delta t_b)=\delta t^2$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(\delta t_a,\delta t_b)=0$. This is a substantive postulate, motivated by the ensemble ontology (\S~\ref{sec:intro}): distinct variants do not share a common clock; there is no strict derivation from it, and what remains irreducible is the premise that the holographic information budget is allocated \emph{per branch} rather than as a single shared register. Within one branch, all degrees of freedom \emph{not} participating in the split share that branch's clock.

Crucially, $\delta t_a$ is not a fluctuation of an external metric field and not environmental noise common to nearby trajectories. It is an \emph{ontic} random shift of proper time; its scale over the interval $t$ is set by $\delta t$ from~\eqref{eq:dt}---the holographic scale of such uncertainty~\cite{ng2005foam}. An environmental source---for example, a difference of gravitational potentials on the interferometer arms---would give a \emph{correlated} shift $\delta t_a$, the closer to common the nearer the branches, and it would cancel in the relative phase; but that is a systematic shift, not noise. The holographic uncertainty attaches to the split subsystem of a branch, not to spatial points, so spatial proximity of branches does not by itself correlate their clocks. Attachment to discrete branches rather than positions is essential: noise independent for arbitrarily close \emph{positions} would mean a random phase field with unbounded gradient---hence a force and momentum diffusion; noise independent for discrete \emph{branches} is pure dephasing without force. Experimental consequences are discussed in \S~\ref{sec:matter_interferometry}.

\section{Decoherence functional}\label{sec:decoherence}

For a composite system we use the decomposition $H = mc^2 + P^2/(2m) + H_{\mathrm{int}}$. For which-path ($E_{\mathrm{br}}=mc^2$ by~(P1)) the phase contribution of the split subsystem gives the relative phase
\begin{equation}
\Delta\varphi=\frac{E_a}{\hbar}\,\delta t_a-\frac{E_b}{\hbar}\,\delta t_b
\approx\frac{mc^2}{\hbar}\,(\delta t_a-\delta t_b),
\label{eq:dphi}
\end{equation}
since $E_a\approx E_b\approx mc^2$ and interference involves the \emph{difference} of contributions, not the absolute phase of each branch.

For a single superposition interval, postulates~(P2)--(P3) fully specify the distribution of $\delta t_a,\delta t_b$ used in the derivation below; the multi-time structure of the noise is discussed in \S~\ref{sec:temporal_noise}. The general formula follows from~\eqref{eq:dphi} and postulates~(P2)--(P3). By~(P3), with $\mathrm{Var}(\delta t_a)=\mathrm{Var}(\delta t_b)=\delta t^2$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}(\Delta\varphi)=\frac{E_a^2+E_b^2}{\hbar^2}\,\delta t^2.
\label{eq:vardphi}
\end{equation}
If the centre of mass branches (which-path),~\eqref{eq:vardphi} gives
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}(\Delta\varphi)=2\,\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\,2}\,\delta t^2,
\qquad \omega_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{mc^2}{\hbar}.
\end{equation}
With \emph{shared} clocks the global phase $mc^2\tau/\hbar$ would cancel in $\Delta\varphi$; with independent clocks (P3) the accumulation $E_{\mathrm{br}}\delta t_a/\hbar$ and $E_{\mathrm{br}}\delta t_b/\hbar$ enters the difference. For internal branches $|0\rangle,|1\rangle$ with a common carrier $|C\rangle$ we take $E_{\mathrm{br}}=\Delta E$ and $\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}=\Delta E/\hbar$; since here $E_a=0$, $E_b=\Delta E$, dephasing is twice as weak as in the symmetric case: $\chi=\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\,2}\,\delta t^2/2$.

Since $\delta t_a,\delta t_b$ are Gaussian (P3), the relative phase $\Delta\varphi$ is also Gaussian and $\langle e^{i\Delta\varphi}\rangle=\exp[-\mathrm{Var}(\Delta\varphi)/2]$; the factor $2$ from the two branches and the factor $1/2$ from Gaussian averaging cancel. In the which-path case, for fringe visibility ($V$ relative to the reference $V_0$):
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\frac{V}{V_0}=e^{-\chi},
\qquad
\chi=\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\,2}\,\delta t^2
=\left(\frac{mc^2}{\hbar}\right)^{2}\!\bigl(\xi\,t_P^2\,t\bigr)^{2/3}.\;}
\label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
The general expression for an arbitrary branch pair is $\chi=(E_a^2+E_b^2)\,\delta t^2/(2\hbar^2)$; for $E_a=E_b=mc^2$ it reduces to~\eqref{eq:chi}. The scaling form $\chi\propto m^2 t^{2/3}$ in a centre-of-mass interferometer follows from the postulates and constitutes the central testable prediction; the overall coefficient carries an $\mathcal O(1)$ uncertainty (through $\xi^{2/3}$, the interpretation of $\delta t$, and the choice of the effective $t$; see \S~\ref{sec:experiment}). The mass-quadratic dependence follows from rest-phase accumulation ($\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}\propto mc^2$) and matches the typical dephasing scaling in models of metric and conformal space-time fluctuations~\cite{bonifacio2009,breuer2009}; in the high-mass interferometry programme, this mass growth of the signal is highlighted as a practical motivation for searching for hypothetical effects~\cite{kialka2022}.

\noindent\textbf{Relation to clock interference and the Compton phase.}
The conceptual framework of ``proper time along each branch'' is already used in quantum-clock interference. In Zych et al., an internal state acts as a clock: a deterministic proper-time difference along the two paths records which-path information in the internal degree of freedom and reduces visibility~\cite{zych2011proper}. Pikovski et al.\ developed this mechanism for a composite object in a gravitational field: an externally imposed potential difference unitarily entangles the centre of mass with the internal-energy distribution, and dephasing appears after tracing out the internal degrees of freedom~\cite{pikovski2015}. In contrast, the present model does not rely on an external potential difference or on a dedicated internal degree of freedom that unitarily encodes which-path information; it introduces the stochastic Planckian scale P2 and branch clock independence (P3), and therefore predicts irreversible ensemble dephasing even for an object in its internal ground state.

Using $\omega_C=mc^2/\hbar$ requires a caveat: in standard quantum mechanics the Compton phase cancels in a closed interferometer~\cite{wolf2011compton}, whereas atom interferometry has been interpreted as providing access to the mass frequency~\cite{lan2013clock}. The present model postulates that, with independent $\delta t_a,\delta t_b$, the rest phase $mc^2$ becomes a random \emph{relative} contribution; P1 fixes $E_{\mathrm{br}}$ relative to the vacuum. Without this premise the central prediction $\chi\propto m^2$ does not arise (see also \S~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\noindent\textbf{Planck mass boundary.}
The decoherence time $t_{\mathrm{dec}}$ is defined by the condition $\chi=1$. From~\eqref{eq:chi}:
\begin{equation}
t_{\mathrm{dec}}=\frac{t_C^3}{\xi\,t_P^2},
\qquad
t_C=\frac{\hbar}{mc^2}.
\label{eq:tdec}
\end{equation}
Setting $t_{\mathrm{dec}}=t_C$, we obtain $t_C=\sqrt{\xi}\,t_P$, i.e.\ the characteristic scale
\begin{equation}
m_\ast=\frac{m_P}{\sqrt{\xi}},
\qquad
m_P=\sqrt{\hbar c/G}\approx 2.2\times10^{-8}\;\text{kg}.
\end{equation}
For $\xi=\mathcal O(1)$ this is of the order of the Planck mass. For $m\gg m_\ast$ we have $t_{\mathrm{dec}}\ll t_C$: inter-branch coherence vanishes over an interval below the uncertainty $\delta t$ from~\eqref{eq:dt} and in principle inaccessible to direct measurement. The mechanism remains pure dephasing without population redistribution or spontaneous emission~\cite{donadi2021}. The ontological consequences of the macroscopic limit---a mixture in the branch basis, objectivity of coherence loss before readout, and distinction from dynamical collapse models---are discussed in \S~\ref{sec:objective_collapse}.

\section{Confrontation with experiment}\label{sec:experiment}

\subsection{Matter-wave interferometry: the primary probe}
\label{sec:matter_interferometry}

By construction~(P1)/(P3) the mechanism is pure rest-phase dephasing without force or momentum diffusion (see~(P3) and \S~\ref{sec:discussion}). The experimental consequence is that non-interferometric bounds on Di\'osi--Penrose (the underground search for spontaneous radiation~\cite{donadi2021}, the Majorana Demonstrator limit~\cite{majorana2022}, and X-ray tests~\cite{piscicchia2024}) do not apply; single atomic clocks are likewise unaffected---the effect exists only \emph{between} branches of a superposition. Matter-wave interferometry of the centre of mass is therefore the most direct laboratory probe of the mechanism with $E_{\mathrm{br}}=mc^2$.

For the same reason, purely optical interferometers provide no direct constraint ($\mathrm d\tau=0$ for a photon); LIGO and the Holometer constrain external metric and spatial models~\cite{chou2017holometer}, but not branch-dependent proper time (BDPT; see \S~\ref{sec:discussion}). Massive mirrors would be subject to the mechanism only in their own centre-of-mass superposition; in the usual classical optical regime they are not, and existing optical null results therefore place no direct bound on $\xi$.

\subsection{Sodium nanoparticles: the Vienna group (2026)}
\label{sec:na_vienna}

\noindent\textbf{Setup parameters.}
We confront Equation~(\ref{eq:chi}) with the sodium-nanoparticle interferometer of the Vienna group~\cite{arndt2025,arndt_arxiv}:
\begin{itemize}
\item clusters of $5000$--$10000$ Na atoms with a mass-distribution centre $m\approx 172$~kDa ($m\approx 2.86\times10^{-22}$~kg);
\item velocity $v\approx 160$~m/s;
\item three gratings of period $d=133$~nm spaced by $L=0.983$~m;
\item a measured fringe visibility $V=0.10\pm0.01$ (second run $0.08\pm0.01$) at $172$~kDa, described by the quantum model with a single global factor $0.78$ for conventional contrast losses (alignment, gravitational and rotational phase averaging, vibrations, thermal and collisional decoherence);
\item a record macroscopicity $\mu=15.45$~\cite{macroscopicity,arndt2025}.
\end{itemize}
The relevant superposition time is the Talbot flight of one section $t=L/v\approx 6.1$~ms (justification of the choice---in \S~\ref{sec:xi_bound}).

Under the sharpened P1 this is not a separate counting rule: the two resolved branches of a rigid cluster are centre-of-mass shifts of the whole body (without change of internal configuration). When their one-atom spatial supports have negligible overlap, $\mathcal D_{\rm Na}=N$ and Equation~\eqref{eq:ebr_dist} gives $E_{\mathrm{br}}=Nm_{\mathrm{at}}c^2=Mc^2$, whence $\chi\propto M^2$. If the overlap is incomplete, $M$ in the formulas below must be replaced by $m_{\mathrm{at}}\mathcal D_{\rm Na}$; the assumption of full branch distinguishability is thus explicit and can be checked against the packet geometry.

\subsection{Numerical estimate and constraint on $\xi$}
\label{sec:xi_bound}
We first fix the effective superposition time. The postulates do not uniquely define the moment at which independent branch clocks ``switch on'' (§~\ref{sec:limitations}): delocalization of the wave function grows already after the first grating (full flight $2L/v\approx12.3$~ms), but distinguishable diffraction orders---branches in the strict sense of~(P1)---form only at the second grating, leaving one section until recombination at the third, $t=L/v\approx6.1$~ms. For an upper bound we take the \emph{minimal} interpretation $t=L/v$: the smaller $t$, the smaller the predicted dephasing and the \emph{weaker} (more conservative) the resulting bound on $\xi$; any broader interpretation of the superposition time would only tighten the limit. With $\omega_C=mc^2/\hbar=2.43\times10^{29}$~s$^{-1}$ (at $m=172$~kDa) and $t_P^2=2.91\times10^{-87}$~s$^2$, Equation~(\ref{eq:chi}) gives
\begin{equation}
\chi\approx 0.41\,\xi^{2/3}\quad (t=L/v\approx 6.1~\text{ms}).
\end{equation}
At $\xi=1$ this is $\chi\approx 0.41$, i.e.\ a visibility deficit of $\approx34\%$.

We use the published raw data and analysis code~\cite{arndt_data}. In the bin $P_2=15.252$~mW three independent scans give
\begin{equation}
V_i=0.0732\pm0.0081,\quad
0.0975\pm0.0070,\quad
0.0780\pm0.0074,
\end{equation}
where the errors are $1\sigma$ from the covariance matrix of a nonlinear sinusoidal fit. For the displayed rounded values, the inverse-variance mean is $\bar V=0.0840$ with a formal error of $0.0043$. For these three scans $\chi^2_{\rm red}=3.08$; following the experiment's procedure for overdispersed bins, we inflate the error by $\sqrt{\chi^2_{\rm red}}$ and obtain $\sigma_{\bar V}=0.0076$.

The official quantum curve at this power gives $V_{\rm QM}=0.1108$ (without conventional losses). The one-sided lower bound on the observed visibility at 95\% is
\begin{equation}
\bar V-1.645\,\sigma_{\bar V}=0.0716.
\end{equation}

We construct the upper bound on $\xi$ strictly. The observed visibility decomposes as $V_{\rm obs}=R_{\rm ord}\,V_{\rm QM}\,e^{-\chi}$, where $R_{\rm ord}\le1$ is the total conventional contrast-loss factor (alignment, vibrations, phase averaging, gas, radiation). Since $R_{\rm ord}$ cannot exceed unity by its physical meaning, regardless of its true value we have $e^{-\chi}\ge V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm QM}$. Attributing \emph{the entire} visibility deficit to our mechanism ($R_{\rm ord}=1$), we obtain
\begin{equation}
e^{-\chi}\ge\frac{0.0716}{0.1108}=0.646,
\qquad
\chi\le0.44,
\end{equation}
and from $\chi=0.41\,\xi^{2/3}$---a strict conservative bound
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\xi\lesssim 1.1\quad(95\%~\text{CL, one-sided}).\;}
\end{equation}
This bound does not depend on the experimental calibration: any real conventional losses ($R_{\rm ord}<1$) could only tighten it. The exclusion threshold corresponds to a visibility deficit $1-0.646\approx35\%$: values of $\xi$ predicting a larger deficit are incompatible with the data at 95\%.

If one trusts the published estimate of conventional losses $R_{\rm ord}=0.78$, the limit tightens: $e^{-\chi}\ge0.0716/(0.78\cdot0.1108)=0.828$, $\chi\le0.188$, whence $\xi<0.31$. But this is a conditional result: at a single mass the product $R_{\rm ord}\,e^{-\chi}$ is degenerate, and the data do not allow separating the new mechanism from underestimated conventional losses; the interpretation of $\delta t$, the choice of effective $t$, and the overall postulate coefficient also remain model systematics.

Summary for the data: at $\xi=1$ the model predicts ($R_{\rm ord}=1$) visibility $0.1108\,e^{-0.41}\approx0.074$ against the observed $0.0840\pm0.0076$---a deviation of only $1.4\sigma$, so $\xi=\mathcal O(1)$ is \emph{not excluded} by the data; the strict bound presses the coefficient to $\xi\lesssim1.1$, the conditional one (with the $0.78$ calibration) to $0.31$. A statistically rigorous limit requires a joint likelihood analysis of the raw scans, the quantum-curve parameters, and all conventional contrast-loss channels.

\subsection{The decisive near-term test}
\label{sec:decisive_test}

The already-measured high visibility $V=0.66\pm0.09$ at $0.4$--$1$~MDa~\cite{arndt2025} does \emph{not} test the mechanism as distinguishable quantum coherence: it was taken in the regime $\lambda_{dB}\lesssim3$~fm, $L\le L_T$ ($L_T\sim d^2/\lambda_{dB}$---the \emph{Talbot length}, the scale of grating self-imaging in the near field of a Talbot--Lau interferometer~\cite{arndt2025,kialka2022}), where the quantum and classical predictions coincide (geometric optics). A discriminating test requires the quantum regime at high mass. The same programme targets $m\sim 1$~MDa with $v\approx 25$~m/s~\cite{arndt2025}. Then even with the conservative $t=L/v\approx 39$~ms and $\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx 1.4\times10^{30}$~s$^{-1}$, Equation~\eqref{eq:chi} at $\xi=1$ gives
\begin{equation}
\chi\approx 47,
\qquad
V/V_0=e^{-\chi}\lesssim 10^{-20}.
\end{equation}
Practically complete loss of interference is robust against $\mathcal O(1)$ uncertainty in the coefficient. The standard quantum model of the setup at the same parameters expects measurable fringes with ordinary contrast. Observing fringes at $\sim\!1$~MDa and $v\approx25$~m/s would falsify the model.

\subsection{Distinguishing signature}
\label{sec:signature}

A visibility deficit alone is not diagnostic, since thermal radiation, collisions with residual gas, and velocity spread also reduce the contrast. The signature of the present mechanism is its scaling $\chi\propto m^2 t^{2/3}$ together with \emph{immunity to shielding}: it cannot be reduced by improving the vacuum or cooling the particle. The experimental discriminator is whether a residual deficit persists after the suppression of all known environmental channels and whether it follows the $m^2$ law. Disappearance of fringes at $\sim\!1$~MDa and $v\approx25$~m/s with scaling $V\propto\exp[-(mc^2/\hbar)^2(\xi t_P^2 t)^{2/3}]$ after excluding environmental losses would be evidence in favour of the model.

An additional discriminator is the dependence on the maximum spatial branch separation $\Delta x$. For momentum-transfer decoherence (collisions and photon emission or scattering), the localization exponent contains factors of the form $1-\operatorname{sinc}(q\Delta x/\hbar)$: it vanishes for unresolved paths, grows as the environment starts to resolve them, and saturates at the event rate once $\Delta x$ exceeds the environment's spatial resolution~\cite{hornberger2004tli}. It would therefore be incorrect to say that \emph{all} environmental channels grow without bound with $\Delta x$. A gravitational gradient usually produces a deterministic geometry-dependent phase; contrast is lost only after averaging over fluctuations of the gradient or trajectories.

The present model is likewise not strictly independent of $\Delta x$ over the entire range. Under the sharpened P1,
\begin{equation}
\chi_{\mathrm{BDPT}}(\Delta x)=
\left[\frac{c^2\sum_s m_s\mathcal D_s(\Delta x)}{\hbar}\right]^2
\bigl(\xi t_P^2t\bigr)^{2/3}.
\label{eq:chi_dx}
\end{equation}
While the packets overlap, $\mathcal D_s(\Delta x)$ grows from zero. The specific signature emerges once the branches are resolved: as $\mathcal D_s\to N_s$, increasing $\Delta x$ further does not change $\chi_{\mathrm{BDPT}}$, producing a plateau that depends only on $m$ and $t$. This is the direct consequence of attaching the noise to distinguishable branches rather than to spatial points.

The test can be performed in one apparatus: at fixed $m$, $v$, superposition time, pressure, and temperature, vary the transverse momentum transfer (preferably the diffraction order; changing the grating period also requires compensating the change in Talbot length) and thereby scan $\Delta x$. The quantity to compare is not raw visibility but the residual exponent
\begin{equation}
\chi_{\mathrm{res}}(\Delta x)=
-\ln\!\left[\frac{V_{\mathrm{obs}}(\Delta x)}{V_{\mathrm{QM}}(\Delta x)}\right]
-\chi_{\mathrm{env}}^{\mathrm{calc}}(\Delta x),
\end{equation}
where $V_{\mathrm{QM}}$ includes coherent dynamics and grating interactions, while $\chi_{\mathrm{env}}^{\mathrm{calc}}$ contains the calculated gas and thermal contributions. The model predicts a constant addition to $\chi_{\mathrm{res}}$ on the distinguishability plateau, whereas unsaturated environmental channels retain a characteristic $\Delta x$ dependence. A flat residual alone is not unique: saturated environmental decoherence and $\Delta x$-independent technical losses can mimic it. The $\Delta x$ scan should therefore be combined with changes in vacuum or temperature and with a test of the $m^2t^{2/3}$ scaling.

\section{Limits on quantum computation}\label{sec:qc}

\subsection{Composite superpositions: flux qubits and atomic NOON states}
\label{sec:many_body}

The unqualified phrase ``energy of the split subsystem'' is particularly hazardous for a superconducting flux qubit. Naively counting all $10^6$--$10^{10}$ electrons carrying superconductivity would give, at the upper end, $E_{\mathrm{br}}\simeq8.2\times10^{-4}$~J and, for $t=100~\mu\mathrm{s}$ and $\xi=1$, $\chi\simeq26.5$; the observed coherence of current states would then exclude the model. A microscopic analysis of the BCS states, however, shows that the near-unit overlap of the one-electron states in the two current branches leaves only a small fraction of occupations changed. An estimate equivalent to the one-particle distinguishability in~\eqref{eq:ebr_dist} gives $\mathcal D_e\simeq42$--$5750$ for the devices considered, rather than the total number of condensate electrons~\cite{korsbakken2009,korsbakken2010}. At $t=100~\mu\mathrm{s}$ this gives $\chi\lesssim9\times10^{-12}$: existing flux qubits are safe for the model. The difference between naive and microscopic counts is not an $\mathcal O(1)$ uncertainty; this is why the distinguishability measure is part of P1.

The same rule separates two often conflated regimes of atom interferometry. A product state
\begin{equation}
\left[(|L\rangle+|R\rangle)/\sqrt2\right]^{\otimes N}
\end{equation}
contains independent one-atom superpositions; observed first-order coherence compares components with $\mathcal D=1$ and acquires the atomic, not collective, energy $mc^2$. In contrast, a NOON state
\begin{equation}
\bigl(|N,0\rangle+|0,N\rangle\bigr)/\sqrt2
\end{equation}
has two collective branches with $\mathcal D=N$ and $E_{\mathrm{br}}=Nmc^2$. For $N=10^5$ ${}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ atoms and $t=10$~ms, Equation~\eqref{eq:chi} predicts $\chi\simeq1.4\times10^3$ at $\xi=1$, i.e.\ practically complete suppression of interference for a massive motional NOON state. This additional falsifiable prediction does not yet conflict with experiment: demonstrated atomic NOON states remain few-particle; for example, Ref.~\cite{chen2010noon} realized a second-order state.

\subsection{Margolus--Levitin: a heuristic comparison}
\label{sec:ml_link}

The decoherence time of the model is the moment when $\chi=1$ and the visibility falls to $e^{-1}$; it is given by Equation~\eqref{eq:tdec}.

The Margolus--Levitin theorem~\cite{margolus1998} limits the speed of orthogonal evolution by the energy \emph{above the ground level} $E_{\mathrm{av}}=\langle H\rangle-E_0$: $\tau_\perp\ge\pi\hbar/(2E_{\mathrm{av}})$, whence $\nu_{\max}=2E_{\mathrm{av}}/(\pi\hbar)$. This is a question \emph{distinct} from branch decoherence: the Margolus--Levitin theorem concerns the unitary speed of state change, not the noise of independent clocks.

At fixed particle number, the term $mc^2$ is proportional to the identity and does not generate orthogonal centre-of-mass evolution; it cancels from $E_{\mathrm{av}}=\langle H\rangle-E_0$. Thus the identification $E_{\mathrm{av}}=E_{\mathrm{br}}=mc^2$ follows neither from the theorem nor from the model's postulates. Only as a formal dimensional illustration, under the additional and nonstandard assumption of that identification, one may write the number of ``ticks'' over the window $t_{\mathrm{dec}}$ as
\begin{equation}
N_{\max}=\nu_{\max}\,t_{\mathrm{dec}}
=\frac{2\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\pi}\cdot\frac{t_C^3}{\xi\,t_P^2}
=\frac{2}{\pi\xi}\left(\frac{t_C}{t_P}\right)^{2}.
\end{equation}
Since $t_C/t_P=m_P/m$, for which-path
\begin{equation}
N_{\max}^{\rm formal}=\frac{2}{\pi\xi}\left(\frac{m_P}{m}\right)^{2}.
\end{equation}
At $m=m_\ast=m_P/\sqrt{\xi}$ this formal quantity is $2/\pi\approx0.64$. It is not a computational-power bound derived from the model. For internal branches, where the transition energy actually generates evolution, the theorem should instead be applied directly using the physical $E_{\mathrm{av}}$, without importing the formula based on $mc^2$.

\subsection{Formal coincidence with holographic scaling}
The same additional identification gives the formal rate $N=(2/\pi)\,t/t_C$. Combining it with the coherence condition $t_{\mathrm{dec}}\ge t$, i.e.\ $t_C\ge(\xi\,t_P^2\,t)^{1/3}$, and taking equality at the boundary gives
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{formal}}(t)=\frac{2}{\pi\,\xi^{1/3}}\left(\frac{t}{t_P}\right)^{2/3}.
\end{equation}
Over one second this is $\approx4.5\times10^{28}$ at $\xi=1$. The exponent $2/3$ coincides with the scaling $N\propto(t/t_P)^{2/3}$ obtained by Ng for a ``space-time-foam computer'' and a black-hole quantum computer~\cite{ng2005foam}, because both estimates use the cubic law $\delta t^3\sim t_P^2t$. This is a meaningful structural analogy, but not an independent derivation of an operation-count bound: without additional physical justification for $E_{\mathrm{av}}=mc^2$, matching exponents does not turn $N_{\mathrm{formal}}$ into a computational limit of the model.

\subsection{Phase noise and the qubit register}

Postulates (P1)--(P3) act on any superposition, including a computational one. For strictly internal levels $|0\rangle,|1\rangle$ with a common one-particle spatial-motional density matrix, P1 gives $E_{\mathrm{br}}=\hbar\omega_q=\Delta E$; the mass of the chip and resonator enters the common $|C\rangle$ and does not enter $\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}$. This statement cannot automatically be transferred to a superposition of opposite persistent currents, for which Equation~\eqref{eq:ebr_dist} applies as discussed in \S~\ref{sec:many_body}. Superconducting resonators storing a single photon for $\sim\!34$~ms with $\gtrsim10^{23}$ electrons in their walls~\cite{milul2023cavity} illustrate the internal case: with $E_{\mathrm{br}}=\Delta E$ of the photon, not the rest energy of the common walls, long-lived coherence is consistent with the model. The random relative phase between a pair of internal branches over time $t$:
\begin{equation}
\sigma(t)=\frac{\sqrt{E_a^2+E_b^2}}{\hbar}\,\delta t
=\omega_q\bigl(\xi\,t_P^2\,t\bigr)^{1/3}
\quad (E_a=0,\;E_b=\hbar\omega_q).
\label{eq:sigmaq}
\end{equation}
For a superconducting qubit ($\omega_q\approx2\pi\times5$~GHz) and $t=1$~s: $\delta t\approx1.4\times10^{-29}$~s, $\sigma\approx4.5\times10^{-19}$~rad (the corresponding dephasing parameter $\chi=\sigma^2/2$). Crucially, the variance~\eqref{eq:sigmaq} is \emph{pairwise} and independent of the number of branches: interference involves only differences of independent $\delta t_a$, and the extremal phase spread over $2^n$ branches grows only as $\sigma\sqrt{2\ln 2^{\,n}}\propto\sigma\sqrt{n}$. For a branch pair differing in the state of $d$ qubits, $\chi\propto d^{\,2}$; in the worst case, when all $n$ qubits differ, the formal limit $n\,\omega_q\,\delta t\lesssim1$ gives $n\lesssim10^{18}$---far beyond practical horizons.

\subsection{Phase distinguishability floor: exact and approximate QFT}
The quantity $\sigma$ sets a \emph{floor} for algorithmic phase rotations: a gate at angle $\theta\lesssim\sigma$ is indistinguishable from the model's intrinsic noise and cannot carry information. An exact quantum Fourier transform on $n$ qubits contains controlled rotations down to $2\pi/2^{\,n}$, and already at $n\gtrsim64$ these angles fall below the floor $\sim10^{-19}$~rad: exact QFT on large registers is forbidden by the model. However, exact QFT is not used in implementations either: by Coppersmith~\cite{coppersmith1994} all rotations with $k>\mathcal O(\log(n/\varepsilon))$ can be \emph{omitted from the circuit} entirely, losing only $\varepsilon$ success probability; the minimum remaining angle $\sim2\pi\varepsilon/n$ is polynomially, not exponentially, small. For Shor's algorithm~\cite{shor1997} at $n=10^4$, $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ this is $\sim10^{-5}$~rad---thirteen orders of magnitude above the floor~\eqref{eq:sigmaq}. Phase-estimation algorithms that read the answer from the position of an interference peak are therefore not limited by the mechanism: it forbids precisely those exponentially small rotations that are excluded from circuits anyway.

\subsection{Grover's algorithm limit}
The case of amplitude amplification~\cite{grover1996} is contrasting. Here there is no QFT and nothing to truncate: each iteration rotates the state vector by a fixed angle $2\theta\approx2/\sqrt{N}$, set by the geometry of the problem, and all $\sim(\pi/4)\sqrt{N}$ iterations form one continuous coherent accumulation without intermediate readouts. The small rotation per step is irremovable: it \emph{is} the algorithm. Comparability with the floor~\eqref{eq:sigmaq} arises when $2/\sqrt{N}\sim\sigma$, i.e.\ at $N\sim(2/\sigma)^2\sim2\times10^{37}\approx2^{124}$: there the signal step is $2^{-61}\approx4\times10^{-19}$~rad. At smaller $N$ (e.g.\ $N=2^{80}$, step $\sim10^{-12}$~rad) the rotation is orders of magnitude above the floor and the mechanism is imperceptible on a single iteration; at larger $N$ the step falls below the floor, and dephasing accumulated over $\sim\sqrt{N}$ iterations becomes critical---although in practice the computation time kills it first. This is consistent with the known structural fragility of the algorithm: with constant noise speed on the oracle, Grover's quadratic speedup is destroyed completely~\cite{regev2008}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Temporal structure of proper-time noise}
\label{sec:temporal_noise}
Postulates P2--P3 specify only the single-interval variance $\delta t$; multi-time covariance, the noise spectrum, and the spin-echo response are not fixed by them. The law $\mathrm{Var}[\delta t(t)]\propto t^{2/3}$ follows from~\eqref{eq:dt}, but does \emph{not} by itself determine the full stochastic process. If Gaussianity of the full process, self-similarity, and stationary increments are additionally imposed, the natural completion is fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent $H=1/3$~\cite{mandelbrot1968fbm}. In that completion, disjoint increments are anticorrelated, the low-frequency spectrum of fractional Gaussian noise behaves as $S(f)\propto|f|^{1/3}$, and echo requires an explicit calculation with the corresponding filter function rather than an automatic cancellation of a quasistatic phase. These spectral properties follow from an additional temporal postulate, not from P2--P3 as presently stated.

\subsection{Limitations and model systematics}\label{sec:limitations}
The central prediction $\chi\propto m^2 t^{2/3}$ follows from the postulates, but the overall coefficient remains incompletely fixed. The number $\xi$ in~\eqref{eq:dt} is specified only as $\mathcal O(1)$; interpreting $\delta t$ as a single-interval variance and choosing the effective superposition time $t$ (e.g.\ $L/v$ versus $2L/v$ in a Talbot--Lau interferometer; see \S~\ref{sec:xi_bound}) enter the $\mathcal O(1)$ uncertainty of the predicted visibility on equal footing with $\xi$. When confronting a single mass, the observed visibility decomposes as $V_{\rm obs}=R_{\rm ord}\,V_{\rm QM}\,e^{-\chi}$; the product $R_{\rm ord}\,e^{-\chi}$ is degenerate, and without varying $m$ or $t$ one cannot uniquely separate the BDPT contribution from underestimated conventional contrast losses. The conditional bound at fixed $R_{\rm ord}=0.78$ is therefore weaker than the strict one-sided estimate that attributes the entire deficit to the mechanism. A statistically robust limit on $\xi$ will require a joint likelihood analysis of the raw scans, the quantum-curve parameters, and all conventional loss channels, not aggregated visibility values alone.

\subsection{Relation to objective collapse}
\label{sec:objective_collapse}
A natural question is how BDPT relates to objective collapse if it extinguishes the relative phase between branches. In standard quantum mechanics, dephasing destroys the off-diagonal elements of the density matrix in the basis of split components. For two branches $|a\rangle,|b\rangle$ at $\chi\gg1$, ensemble averaging over independent $\delta t_a,\delta t_b$ yields the effective state
\begin{equation}
\rho \approx |c_a|^2|a\rangle\langle a| + |c_b|^2|b\rangle\langle b|,
\end{equation}
without coherent terms $c_a c_b^*\,|a\rangle\langle b|+\text{h.c.}$ The loss of phase and passage to a mixed state is not reducible to the observer's incomplete knowledge of the phase: by~(P3) the noise is an \emph{ontic} independent shift of proper time per branch, not the recording of which-path information in an environment.

In the ensemble reading (\S~\ref{sec:intro}) each branch is a genuine variant of the system and $|c_i|^2$ are the variant weights. For $\chi\gg1$ there is no observable interference between variants, and the mixture with the same weights becomes \emph{operationally} indistinguishable from the realization of one variant with probability $|c_i|^2$. The definite outcome remains unknown until registration, but coherent superposition of alternatives as a physical resource disappears \emph{objectively}---before and independently of readout---over a time $\lesssim t_{\mathrm{dec}}$. In this sense the macroscopic limit of BDPT implements objective collapse in the branch basis.

The mechanism differs from dynamical collapse models (Di\'osi--Penrose and others) in its channel: the populations $|c_i|^2$ are not redistributed by a jump and there is no spontaneous emission~\cite{donadi2021}; it is pure rest-phase dephasing. Yet the destruction of inter-branch coherence and the passage to a mixture is precisely the link to objectivist programmes. No separate nonlinear term in the state equation is postulated: ``collapse'' follows from the ensemble ontology, independent branch clocks, and the P2 scale. Schr\"odinger's cat at $m\gg m_\ast$ illustrates the limit: the amplitudinal record ``alive/dead'' remains, but interference between branches vanishes over $t_{\mathrm{dec}}\ll t_C$; what remains is a statistical mixture of variants with weights $|c_i|^2$.

\subsection{Distinction from other decoherence mechanisms}
The mechanism must be distinguished from nearby sources of interference loss. In quantum-clock interference, a deterministic or potential-induced proper-time difference records which-path information through internal degrees of freedom or an external gravitational gradient~\cite{zych2011proper,pikovski2015}; BDPT is not reducible to such unitary readout and does not require a potential difference, but introduces a stochastic Planckian scale with independent $\delta t_a,\delta t_b$. An environment with momentum transfer (gas, thermal radiation, photon scattering) creates position-dependent noise, force, and momentum diffusion and is usually suppressed by improving the vacuum or cooling; the characteristic dependence on $\Delta x$ is described by localization factors~\cite{hornberger2004tli} (see \S~\ref{sec:signature}). Dynamical Di\'osi--Penrose collapse models predict population redistribution and spontaneous emission~\cite{donadi2021}, to which the present model is \emph{transparent}: it is pure rest-phase dephasing without additional channels. Spatially attached implementations of Planckian uncertainty (correlated shear noise, external metric fluctuations) are constrained by devices such as the Holometer and LIGO-like detectors~\cite{chou2017holometer}; BDPT attaches to discrete branches, not to points, and does not act on a massless carrier. Finally, in standard quantum mechanics the Compton phase cancels in a closed interferometer~\cite{wolf2011compton}; the present model does not settle that debate but postulates that, with independent branch clocks, the rest phase $mc^2$ becomes a random \emph{relative} contribution (see \S~\ref{sec:decoherence})---a separate and falsifiable premise.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Tying the K\'arolyh\'azy fluctuation to proper time and postulating independent branch clocks yields a decoherence law (for which-path, $\xi=\mathcal O(1)$)
\begin{equation}
\boxed{V/V_0=\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{mc^2}{\hbar}\right)^{2}\!\bigl(\xi\,t_P^2\,t\bigr)^{2/3}\right],}
\end{equation}
where interference involves the \emph{difference} of branch phase contributions and $mc^2$ is the energy of the split carrier. The scaling $m^2 t^{2/3}$ is immune to electromagnetic shielding; sodium interferometry~\cite{arndt2025} gives a strict one-sided 95\% bound $\xi\lesssim1.1$ (the entire contrast deficit attributed to the mechanism; conditionally, with the conventional-loss normalization $0.78$, $\xi\lesssim0.31$). For $\xi=\mathcal O(1)$ the characteristic scale $m_\ast=m_P/\sqrt{\xi}$ is of the order of the Planck mass; for $m\gg m_\ast$ decoherence occurs over $t_{\mathrm{dec}}\ll t_C$, below the K\'arolyh\'azy distinguishability limit for proper time. The model is falsifiable by a test at $\sim\!1$~MDa and $v\approx25$~m/s: either fringes of the standard quantum model, or suppression with the $m^2 t^{2/3}$ scaling.
